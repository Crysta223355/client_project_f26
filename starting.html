Starting Your Experience

Begin with Shared Expectations

The beginning of a project sets the tone for the work that follows. Use early conversations to understand the partner's goals, confirm what the team can deliver, and decide how everyone will communicate and make decisions.

Establish the Project Foundation

Confirm the Purpose and Scope

Restate the problem in language the team and partner understand. Identify the intended users, desired outcome, major deliverables, available information, important constraints, and work that will remain outside the project.

Do not assume that every detail is final after the first meeting. Record open questions and identify who can answer them.

Define Team Roles

  • Assign an owner for each immediate task or deliverable.
  • Choose who will organize meetings and document decisions.
  • Discuss how work will be reviewed before it is shared.
  • Make responsibilities visible so the workload can be adjusted when needed.

Agree on Communication

  • Choose the main communication channel for the team and partner.
  • Set a realistic schedule for meetings and progress updates.
  • Decide how quickly team members should respond.
  • Identify who should be included when a decision changes the scope or timeline.

Use a Project Kickoff Checklist

  1. Introduce team members and explain each person's role.
  2. Ask the partner to describe the need, audience, and desired outcome.
  3. Review expected deliverables, milestones, and important dates.
  4. Confirm how files, feedback, questions, and decisions will be shared.
  5. Identify immediate next steps, owners, and due dates.
  6. Send a written summary and invite corrections.
