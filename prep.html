Preparing Your Experience

Prepare Before the Project Begins

Engaged learning brings academic knowledge into real organizations and communities. Before work begins, learn about the project context, consider what your team can contribute, and identify what you still need to learn.

Understand the Context

A community-engaged project is different from a controlled classroom exercise. Technical choices interact with people, organizational cultures, available resources, and community priorities.

Approach the work as a collaborative learner. Listen carefully, ask questions, and avoid assuming that the first proposed solution is the best response to the partner's needs.

Questions to Consider

  • What do I hope to learn from this experience?
  • Who may be affected by the project and its outcomes?
  • What assumptions are we making about the partner, community, or problem?
  • Which skills and perspectives are represented on our team?
  • Which skills or perspectives may be missing?
  • What would a useful and mutually beneficial outcome look like?

Prepare as a Team

Set Learning Goals

Identify the technical, professional, interpersonal, and ethical skills each team member wants to develop. Clear learning goals make it easier to choose meaningful responsibilities and reflect on progress later.

Assess Team Readiness

  • List each person's relevant skills, interests, and availability.
  • Discuss responsibilities and constraints before assigning work.
  • Identify knowledge gaps that may require research or support.
  • Record questions that should be discussed with the partner.

Use a Preparation Process

  1. Reflect on the experiences and assumptions that shape your approach.
  2. Research the organization, community, project history, and stakeholders.
  3. Set individual and team learning goals.
  4. Compare the team's capacity with the likely project needs.
  5. Prepare questions before confirming scope or deliverables.
