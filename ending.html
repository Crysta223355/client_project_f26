Ending the Experience

Close the Project with Care

A final presentation is only one part of a responsible closeout. Confirm that the partner can access, understand, maintain, and make decisions about the work after the student team steps away.

Create a Useful Handoff

Package the Final Work

  • Organize final files with clear names and remove outdated duplicates.
  • Describe the purpose, status, and limitations of each deliverable.
  • Provide setup, access, maintenance, and troubleshooting instructions.
  • Transfer ownership or permissions only to approved partner contacts.
  • List unfinished work and recommendations without presenting them as commitments.

Confirm the Closeout

Review the handoff with the partner instead of sending files without context. Confirm receipt, answer questions, record final feedback, and agree on when the team's access to partner systems or data should end.

Reflect on Learning and Impact

Reflect Individually

  • What changed in your understanding of the problem or community?
  • Which contribution are you most proud of, and why?
  • Where did you struggle, adapt, or ask for help?
  • How will this experience change the way you approach future work?

Describe Your Contribution

Use a simple story: describe the situation, name your responsibility, explain the action you took, and provide evidence of the result. Be specific about your own role while recognizing the work of teammates and partners.

Protect confidential information. Ask the partner before publishing project details, screenshots, data, or identifiable stories in a portfolio.

Final Closeout Checklist

  • The partner has the final files and documentation.
  • Permissions and ownership have been transferred.
  • Sensitive information is stored or deleted as agreed.
  • The team has collected feedback and thanked the partner.
  • Each student has recorded learning while the experience is fresh.
